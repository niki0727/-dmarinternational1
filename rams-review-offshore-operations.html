RAMS Review for Offshore Operations

Our RAMS review marine service helps offshore teams turn complex procedures into executable plans. We perform offshore RAMS review and method statement review for marine operations, challenging assumptions early so risk controls, responsibilities, and decision gates are clear before mobilisation and field execution.

Talk to an expert See case studies

Value before mobilisation

Clients involve us when RAMS maturity is uneven, when multiple contractors must align quickly, or when insurer/client comments need structured close-out.

What we assess

  • Offshore RAMS review for technical and operational consistency
  • Method statement review marine operations with clear hold points
  • Interface checks between risk register, procedures, and execution roles

Deliverables

  • Comment matrix with practical corrections and rationale
  • Close-out tracker with ownership and evidence status
  • Readiness summary for management and assurance stakeholders

Why RAMS reviews fail

  • Field execution drift from unclear instructions
  • Repeated document rework cycles and approval delay
  • Uncontrolled interface risk between contractors

Relevant sectors

  • Offshore wind and cable campaigns
  • Ports, heavy-lift, and nearshore operations
  • Oil and gas and decommissioning work scopes

Common documentation gaps

  • Engineering authoring teams and marine operations teams
  • Client / contractor RAMS alignment
  • Onshore planning versus offshore reality interface

Standards and guidance considered

  • DNV and IMCA method and risk guidance
  • Client HSEQ and assurance frameworks
  • Contractual technical authority requirements

FAQs

How detailed should RAMS be before review?
Even draft RAMS benefit from early review because major structure issues are cheaper to fix before final sign-off.
Can you review only critical sections?
Yes. We can prioritise high-risk operations first to support urgent schedule needs.
Do you provide full rewrites?
We provide structured comments and practical corrections; full rewrites can be supported when agreed.
