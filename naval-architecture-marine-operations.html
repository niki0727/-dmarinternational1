Naval Architecture for Marine Operations

Our naval architecture support helps developers, EPCIs, MWS firms, and shipowners verify stability, ballast logic, and structural interfaces before offshore execution. We focus on practical outputs that reduce rework risk and improve readiness when campaign timing and vessel costs are under pressure.

Talk to an expertSee case studies

When clients involve us

Clients involve us before transport and installation commitment, during late engineering changes, or when marine teams need independent technical challenge on stability and sequence assumptions.

What we review

  • Intact and damaged stability calculations for operation envelopes
  • Ballast and de-ballast sequence assumptions for execution stages
  • Deck loading, support reactions, and temporary works interfaces

Deliverables

  • Technical findings report with prioritised close-out actions
  • Readiness notes linked to vessel and operation limitations
  • Decision-ready summary for engineering and marine leadership

Typical risks reduced

  • Late rework caused by unstable loading assumptions
  • Offshore delay from unresolved ballast sequence conflicts
  • Interface failures between engineering and marine execution teams

Relevant sectors

  • Offshore wind transport and installation campaigns
  • Subsea and heavy marine construction scopes
  • Oil and gas, decommissioning, and project cargo operations

Common vessel / project interfaces

  • Naval architecture package and marine spread interface
  • Port, yard, and vessel handover interfaces
  • EPCI engineering assumptions versus offshore operating limits

Standards and guidance considered

  • DNV marine operation and stability-related guidance
  • Relevant class, flag, and project technical criteria
  • IMCA practices where marine operations interfaces apply

FAQ

Yes. We can prioritise critical-path calculations and readiness findings first.
